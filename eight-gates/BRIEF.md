# Creative brief — The Eight Gates

> Note on sourcing: this brief was written from desk knowledge, without a live Perplexity run. Run the step-1 research prompt to confirm the competitor positioning before using it in a client presentation. The creative recommendations hold up either way.

## 1. Tone and visual style that works

**Tone: calm confidence, not hype.** AI-innovation brands that land with B2B buyers (R&D heads, IP counsel, CTOs) talk like a precise instrument and avoid sounding like a magic trick. Patent buyers are allergic to overclaiming: "we invent for you" triggers legal skepticism, while "we find what's patentable faster" builds trust.

- **Voice:** short declarative lines. One idea per sentence. Present tense.
- **Visual language:** dark, high-contrast environments with a single disciplined accent color (this spot uses cyan for the machine and gold for the human or valuable). Volumetric light, architecture at monumental scale, slow camera.
- **Avoid:** glowing brains, humanoid robots, blue binary rain, handshake stock footage. Every competitor uses them, so they read as generic.
- **B2C crossover:** keep one human moment, such as a hand or a face lit by the gate. The audience has to feel that a person owns the result.

## 2. Gate / portal metaphors and tagline territory

| Metaphor | Why it works | Risk |
|----------|--------------|------|
| **Gate = discipline** | Each gate is a specialist lens on the same problem | Can feel like a corporate org chart. Fix this by giving each gate a distinct color and motion |
| **Passing through = transformation** | An idea enters raw and leaves as an invention | — |
| **Vault / archive** | Patents are about the record: what exists, what doesn't | Too static on its own |
| **Lighting up / igniting** | Discovery as a visible event | Overused if every frame glows. Save full ignition for the climax |
| **Convergence beam** | Eight disciplines resolve into one answer (the seal) | — |

Tagline candidates:
- **"Where ideas become patents."** (recommended end line: plain and ownable)
- "Every invention has a way in."
- "Eight ways in. One way forward."
- "Walk through."
- "The undiscovered, discovered."

## 3. How multi-capability AI stacks get shown in ads

The common pattern is a **capability montage**: a fast cut of logos or features, then a unifying line. It usually fails because the viewer sees a feature list with no sense of a system. What works better:

1. **One space, many rooms.** All capabilities live in a single physical world (here, the hall), so the audience reads them as one product.
2. **One sentence per capability**, each with a verb that *does* something (sketches, renders, hunts, builds).
3. **A visible handoff.** Each capability visibly feeds the next. In this spot, light travels gate to gate.
4. **A single payoff object.** The montage needs somewhere to land. Here it lands on the patent seal.

## Recommended creative angles

1. **"Hidden in plain sight"** *(chosen)*. Inventions already exist as possibilities and need to be found. The gates are search instruments. Open in darkness, end in light. This angle fits the patent-discovery positioning best and is the most defensible claim.
2. **"The relay."** One raw spark is passed gate to gate like a baton and changes form at each stop (sketch, render, film, text, code), arriving as a patent. It works well for a 15s cutdown.
3. **"The empty map."** A world map of every patent ever filed, rendered as light, with dark gaps. The gates fly out to fill the gaps. This is a strong B2B/data angle and could serve as a follow-up spot.

## Top visual metaphors for the spot

1. A monumental hall with eight gates in an arc (the key visual)
2. Light traveling gate to gate as the handoff
3. Each gate's icon animating once as the camera passes
4. All eight igniting at once and converging into a single beam
5. The beam forming a patent seal: gold, embossed, with a serial number ticking into place
